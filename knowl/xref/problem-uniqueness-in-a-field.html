Problem 1. Uniqueness in a field.

Let \(\of{ F , + , \cdot } \) be a field
🔗

(a)

Show that the additive identity element \(0 \in F \) is unique.
🔗
🔗

(b)

Show that the multiplicative identity element \(1 \in F \) is unique.
🔗
🔗

(c)

Show that additive inverses are unique; that is, given elements \(x , y , z \in F \text{,}\) show that if
\begin{equation*} x + y = x + z = 0\text{,} \end{equation*}
then \(y = z \text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(d)

Show that multiplicative inverses are unique; that is, given elements \(x , y , z \in F \text{,}\) show that if
\begin{equation*} x \cdot y = x \cdot z = 1\text{,} \end{equation*}
then \(y = z \text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
