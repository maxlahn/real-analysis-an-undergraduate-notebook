Problem 2. Sign in an ordered field.

Let \(\of{ F , + , \cdot , \leq } \) be an ordered field. As in the case of the rational numbers \(\Q \) and the real numbers \(\R \text{,}\) we can define the sign of an element of \(F \) by comparison with the additive identity element \(0 \text{:}\)
  • Positivity.
    A non-zero element \(x \in F \setminus \set{ 0 } \) is called positive if \(0 \leq x \text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Negativity.
    A non-zero element \(x \in F \setminus \set{ 0 } \) is called negative if \(x \leq 0 \text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Zero.
    The additive identity element \(0 \in F \) is not positive or negative.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

(a)

Let \(x \in F \text{.}\) Show that if \(x \neq 0 \text{,}\) then \(x \) and its additive inverse \(-x \) have opposite sign.
🔗
Hint.
Add \(-x \) to the inequality defining the sign of \(x \text{.}\)
