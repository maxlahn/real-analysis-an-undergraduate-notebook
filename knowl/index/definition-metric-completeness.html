Definition 3.6.4. Metric completeness.

Let \(U \subseteq X \) be a subset of a metric space \(\of{ X , \rho } \text{.}\)
  • Completeness.
    The subset \(U \) is called complete in \(X \) if every Cauchy sequence of points in \(U \) converges in \(X \) to a limit in \(U \text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Incompleteness.
    The subset \(U \) is called incomplete in \(X \) if some Cauchy sequence of points in \(U \) does not converge in \(X \) to a limit in \(U \text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
A metric space \(\of{ X , \rho } \) is called complete (resp. incomplete) if \(X \) is complete (resp. incomplete) in \(X \text{.}\) A complete normed vector space is called a Banach space.
